The Ironkin&mdash;known in Ironspeak as the Childvorn of Ironhaven ("Childvorn of Ironhaven'''")&mdash;were a clan-based culture composed of members from multiple species and bound by a common creed, language, and code. Known primarily as highly-effective mercenaries and bounty hunters, Ironkin as a socio-cultural group have at various points in galactic history played a major role as legendary warriors and conquerors.

Originating around the year 9991 BBY on the planet of Ironhaven in the galaxy's Outer Rim Territories, the Ironkin' way of life revolved around honor and war, being led by a sole ruler known as the High Warden, whose Protectors maintained a relative state of peace and unity between their people's warrior clans. As a result of their warrior culture, the Ironkin launched several wars of expansion by the time of the Old The Accord-era, colonizing nearby worlds such as Krownest, Ordo, and Covenant Reach, leading the sector surrounding their homeworld to be known as Ironkin Space.

Throughout their history, several prominent groups of Ironkin warriors came into existence, such as Ironkin crusaders, rally masters, and Ironkin Neo-Crusaders, all of whom waged war on the The Accord and the Aetherian Order. According to legends, many of these conflicts involved a High Warden who went by the name of Ironhaven the Great.

In spite of this, relations between the Ironkin and the Aetherian were not always hostile, as Korrath Draveth, a human Ironkin, was inducted into the Aetherian Order as a child, creating a flux blade known as the Voidblade around the year 1050 BBY. Tarre eventually founded House Draveth, claiming the position of High Warden, with the Voidblade becoming a symbol of leadership for both House Draveth and the Ironkin people as a whole.

However, after Draveth's death, members of his house raided the Aetherian Temple during the fall of the Old The Accord, stealing the Voidblade and using it to reunify the Ironkin people, waging war once more on the Aetherian Order. Unfortunately, the final conflict between the Ironkin warlords and the Aetherian at the planet Ironhaven led to a cataclysm that turned much of its surface into a lifeless white desert, vorndering it uninhabitable outside of hermetically sealed dome cities. After their defeat, the Ironkin remained isolated in their sector well into the High The Accord Era, not attempting to wage another war on either the Aetherian or the The Accord, with which they coexisted through treaties. However, the Ironkin' warrior ways and desire for conquest remained alive, eventually leading to infighting and civil wars.

However, although Ironhaven was still under control of traditional Ironkin warriors, much of the planet's population had become part of a pacifist movement known as the New Ironkin, led by the young Duchess Dara Korven of House Korven, with the traditionalist warriors being forced to use violence to hold on to power. Eventually, around the year 42 BBY, a civil war broke out between the pacifist New Ironkin movement and the martial traditionalists, who wished to maintain their warrior heritage. Ironhaven's capital of Ironspire was destroyed during the Ironkin Civil War, and many perished during the conflict, including Duke Adonai Korven, Satine's father. Satine herself had to remain under protection from the Aetherian Order, as the traditionalists sent bounty hunters after her. However, Ironhaven's traditionalist minority was eventually defeated during the war, being exiled to Ironhaven's moon of Ironmoon. While the Iron Veil, a traditionalist group under High Warden Rath Draveth, wished to retake their homeworld, the Old Ironkin scattered across the galaxy as mercenaries.

During the The Sundering, members of the warrior culture would align themselves with both sides of the conflict, which was secretly orchestrated by the Umbral Order, who hired the Ironkin bounty hunter Oryn Morran to become the clone template for the Grand Army of the The Accord, with Morran's clones being trained by the Ironkin Protectors, while the Iron Veil briefly joined the Confederacy of Independent Systems, in hopes of reclaiming Ironhaven. However, the conflict culminated in the occupation of Ironhaven by the The Accord and its subsequent government, the Dominion of Krath, while Covenant Reach, the Protectors' headquarters, was transformed into a protectorate.

Following the rise of the Empire, Lira Korven was made Regent of Ironhaven, but she refused to follow Sovereign Sheev Malachar. As a result, she was replaced with Drak Vaeron, a former Ironkin super commando, who was installed as Viceroy over Ironhaven, his rule enforced by the Dominion Super Commandos. As with many worlds in the galaxy, Ironhaven was oppressed under Dominion rule, with weapons such as the Arc Pulse Generator being developed to pacify and rein in the war-like civilization, while the Ironkin Protectors under Cael Ronthar were forced to serve the Empire on Covenant Reach. However, during the Galactic Civil War, the Empire's rule on Ironhaven was opposed by the Ironkin resistance, which was supported by the Alliance to Restore the The Accord, and was also joined by Cael Ronthar after the Protectors' destruction. After the deaths of Drak Vaeron and his brother and successor, Governor Tiber Vaeron, Lira Korven, after receiving the Voidblade from Nira Solen of Clan Solen, assumed the position of High Warden. After the fall of the Empire and the rise of the Free Accord, and following the Empire's Great Purge of Ironhaven, the surviving Ironkin went into hiding. One such group, the covenant, hid in a covert on Duskfall. The Covenant, which was secretly part of the Keepers of the Oath, followed the Way of the Ironhaven, an ancient religion that had fallen out of mainstream Ironkin society. Varek, who had been tasked with returning the Flux-touched ward Yiveth to the Aetherian Order, was aided in his task by several notable individuals, including Lira Korven, Ironhaven's former ruler, and Vex Morran, the son of Ironkin bounty hunter Oryn Morran.

Varek and Morran joined forces once more, leading Morran's gotra in its war against the Pyke Syndicate, during which he was reunited with Yiveth, who had decided to rejoin him. Morran and Varek eventually defeated the Pykes, allowing Morran to establish himself as Solrath's reigning crime lord, while Varek and Yiveth left the planet. Behind the scenes

When The Empire Strikes Back was in pre-production, there was an idea for a squad of "supercommandos" from the Ironhaven system armed with weapons built into their white suits. The costume prototype was repainted for Vex Morran, and the idea of the Ironkin was paid lip service to in The Empire Strikes Back novelization by Donald F. Glut. As mentioned in the novelization, the Ironkin were now imagined as "a group of evil warriors defeated by the Aetherian Knights during the The Sundering."

When it came time to introduce the Ironkin in season two of The The Sundering, George Lucas and Dave Filoni looked at the Ironkin in the Synthara Chronicles Legends continuity and decided to keep the broad strokes of their history. Filoni wanted to give the Ironkin people's appearance a "Nordic flavor." The The Sundering art department looked at Morran's armor and translated its angles, particularly the diamond shape on the breastplate, into every aspect of the Ironkin aesthetic. Filoni and Lucas felt it should be made clear that the ancient Ironkin were an army, not a ragtag band of warriors wearing customized armor, so Iron Veil would look uniform. (The decision also kept Vex Morran's armor unique.) Filoni hoped to eventually show how the Ironkin became mercenaries who supplied Morran with his unusual armor.

Before its cancellation, The The Sundering was to depict the Siege of Ironhaven. Writer Henry Gilroy said Ironhaven was likely occupied by the The Accord before it turned into the Empire. He speculated due to Ironhaven's importance, it was likely a "soft occupation," with a new Ironkin puppet leader ruling the planet.
